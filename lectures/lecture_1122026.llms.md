# Why do States Join International Organizations

When states share a common problem, they are incentivized to join international organizations of other states with those problems. The benefits of the organizations that these states join can be enjoyed collectively by the states that join them. However, this does not mean that each state contributes evenly.

There are two types of goods that are shared by states in a collective international organization.

## Private Goods

With goods that are private, it can said to encourage rivalry if one states consumption affects the amount another state can consume.

Public goods are non-rivalrous goods (such as clean air) since either state can consume as much as possible and it does not affect the other state. These goods are considered non excludable goods in other words you cannot prevent someone from consuming this good.

## Club Goods

Club goods are excludable when their access is controlled. Only some can enjoy the benefits of this good. A good that is Excludable but non-rivalrous, it’s considered a club good. (Such as GPU’s currently) Or like a shared toll road, if you pay the toll you can use the road.

If a good is non-excludable and non-rivalrous it’s a public good (like clean air).

Non-excludable rivalrous goods are considered common-pool resources, this could be something like shared river water between two states.

## Bretton Woods System

At the end of WWII, 44 allied states met in the US to create the foundations that the IMF and World Bank. These institutions were intended to serve as economic pillars of global stability and to prevent another global depression as well as support reconstruction and development.

**IMF (International Monetary Fund)**

The intention here was to create monetary stability and to provide external financing.

The goal was monitiring the montary system and lending reserve currencies to countries experiencing financing difficulties.

**World Bank**

The World Bank assisted with reconstruction and development by financing reconstruction in war-torn countries and supporting poverty reduction and development.

The US gold standard collapsed under Nixon during the Vietnam war. In order to prevent stagflation Nixon removed the gold standard and begain printing dollars. THe US would have had to had a certain amount of gold to back a larger amount of dollars but this would have been extremely costly. Countries were demanding gold in exchange for dollars and so in 1971 Nixon abandoned the fixed exchange rate system to the floating exchange rate system.

OPEC countries are those countries that produce a large amount of oil, they figured their oil was valued less because the value of the dollar was less. If the dollar is matched to the value of gold there is a fixed value. But since it’s not there is a depreciation of the dollar value over time.

To compensate for the loss of the dollar value comapred to their oil, these oil companies had to increase the price of their oil significantly in order to cover the potential costs of the loss of the value of the dollar, this created oil shocks and further stagflation.

### IMF LEnding, Moral Hazard, and Conditionality

**Balance of Payment Crisis**

When a country experiences a balance of payments crisis, foreign reserves may fall and external payments become difficult to meet. An IMF loaan can be used to provide temporary financing and prevent an immediate default. The government therefore gets some time to adjust.

The money for an IMF loan comes from member countries that contribute to the IMF. The IMF is sustained by members contributing to it’s funds. Countries that make larger contributions they tend to have a larger voting share. They can therefore decide who does and does not get these IMF loans.

THe IMF requires 85% of voting rights to agree in order to act, the US has 16.1% of voting rights making it’s vote unilateral. Without it the IMF cannot act.
