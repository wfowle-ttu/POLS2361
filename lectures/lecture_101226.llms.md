# Diversionary Conflict Theory

Leaders have an incentive to iniate conflict to divert attention from domestic problems.

Therefore domestic weakness can increase conflict. This can lead leaders to “play tough”.

## Strategic Conflict Avoidance

If an opposing state knows that because of domestic weakness a leader is playing tough, the opposing state is incentivized to avoid conflict with that state.

Therefore domestic weakenss can serve as bargaining leverage. But some others argue that it actually leads to vulnerabilities in terms of international bargaining. So which is it?

According to the Diversionary Conflict Theory the leader who is weak domestically has a more credible threat.

# Putnam’s Two Level Games

International negotiation cannot be seperated cleanly from domestic politics. National leaders must often satisfy two audiences at the same time.

At the first level, Internation Bargaining has multiple feasible outcomes. Within those outcomes leaders seek an agreement that satisfy their relevant domestic actors, that domestic level is level 2.

Agreements reached at level one only matter if they are later ratified by level 2.

## Win Sets and Ratifications

A win set is a set of possible international agreements that can obtain domestic approval. The overlap between the win sets of the negotiating states determines whether a mutually ratifiable bargain exists.

When two countries have large win sets, then it it likely they will be able to come to an agreement, this however, reduces leverage because both parties know that each side has room to concede.

With a small win set, this makes coming to an agreement harder. Likewise a smaller win set means that a country has less incentive to give leeway to the opposing negotiation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Determinants of Win Set Size

- Domestic preferences and coalitions: A wide distribution of acceptable outcomes can enlarge the win set. Deep polarization or powerful veto groups have the ability to shrink the size.

- Political Institutions: Ratification rules, legilatures, courts, coalition governements, etc. all affect the size of the win set.

## Bargaining Implications & Strategy

THe following list are some ways a leader might expand their win set”

1.  Coalition Building: Expand domestic support
2.  Side payments: Compensate domestic groups that expect to lose from the bargain.
3.  Issue Linkage: Combine several issues so gains on one dimension offset losses on another.
4.  Domestic constraint as leverage: Emphasize limimted room for concession.
5.  Reverberation: Use international developments to reshape domestic preferences.

## Applications in Security and Conflict

- Domestic ratification requirements prevent leaders from making international commitments that lack political support.
- Visible constraints can clarify which bargains are feasible and reduce false expectations.
- International partners may treat a domestically difficult concession as a more credible sign of commitment.
