# Economic Sanctions

When two states have conflicting interests war is not always the preferred or optimal solution. But it still maybe unpopular for a leader domestically to do nothing. Attacking a state militarily is costly and risky therefore *economic sanctions* maybe instead a preferred method to take action and satisfy domestic yearn for action.

Economic sanctions are considered a coercive foreign policy tool as the goal is to change the other state’s behavior. If the cost of such a policy then the target may reconsider it’s behavior.

Purposes of Economic Sanction:

1.  Deterrance

The goal is to prevent other countries from taking a certain action. The goal of deterrance can only be realized in the future. If a country expects another state to show a certain behavior in the future, then a state might levy sanctions to deter that other country from taking that action. Deterrance can be either direct or indirect. Direct deterrance means a state is protecting it’s own interests from the other states behavior in the future. Indirect deterrance is to protect a protege country, in this case a state might make a threat to protect a nation that is allied to itself.

A popular method of deterrance is a show of force, but in a global economy, economic pressure tends to be more effective.

2.  Compellance

Compellance is not about deterring other states behavior. It is about changing the current behavior of other states. For example, the US might levy sanctions against Iran for their ongoing production of nuclear weapons. Economic sanctions are a popular compellance method as the alternative is military action.

## Types of Economic Sanctions

Countries implementing sanctions are called ‘senders’. On the other end, the country that is receiving sanctions is considered the ‘target’.

Trade restrictions, financial restrictions:

1.  Trade

Import bans, export controls, shipping limits, are examples of trade restrictions. The idea is to weaken the other states’ production capacity and access to goods and services. Trade restrictions often create costs across the target economy.

2.  Financial Sanctions

These are becoming more popular as modern ecnomies depend on financial assets, banking systems, and access to currency markets. The sanctions can heavily reduce the targets economic flexibility.

3.  Travel Bans

Banning travel of foreign peoples to a given country can also be a form of sanctions. For example, Russian dignitaries are not allowed to enter certain countires.

4.  Military Sanctions

Military sanctions such as arms embargos, restrictions in military assistance. With an arms embargo a country who might be desperate for weaponry might be targeted with militarty sanctions when other states ban the export of miltiary assets to that country. Additionally, a country who wants to deliver weapons to other countries might be targeted with military sanctions if that country is prevented from exporting weapons.

Restrictions on military assistance can apply to countries whose militaries depend on outside assistance. For example, Pakistan’s military depends on the US for anti terrorism efforts. Reducing or removing militar aid is a form of military sanctions. If a target country is heavily dependant on the sender countries weapon system then they can experience military sanctions when the sender country restsricts assistance/training with those systems.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Most sanctions are implemented by three entities in the world. 60-70% of unilateral economic sanctions were implemented by the United States. Then the UN and European Union also implement many economic sanctions.

If the sender is not economically powerful enough to impose economic sanctions then the sender is likely not willing to implement economic sanctions. A sender’s economy must be strong enough to implement economic sanctions. Economic sanctions implement costs on both the target economy but also on the sender’s economy. For example, the US ban on exports of certain computer chips to China means that China will not benefit from those advanced chips, but likewise US firms that produce these chips will have to accept less revenue since they are effectively cut off from a large market of potential customers.

These economic costs can translate into political cost. If foreign policy is expected to hurt domestic economics then the voting base of the the leader in power may be less likely to support that leader since their economic sanctions are imposing costs on their positions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Many economic sanctions are designed to undermine the leadership of the target country. For example, the citizens of a target country may not have access to the goods and services they need, likewise, shortages of those goods and services will cause prices in the target country to rise.

If the target country is an importer of inelastic goods then countries will have a tougher time sending sanctions related to those goods.

‘inelastic’ goods are goods that are necessities to all peoples.

In autocratic regimes, those regimes are able to survive the economic costs of sanctions even though the public may have a negative view of the leaderships’ behavior that led to sanctions. Autocratic regimes are likely to participate in black market activity.

Democratic leaders are more accountable for economic costs and are more likely to experience higher political costs from economic sanctions.

Economic sanctions do not substantially increase the failure of leadership in autocratic regimes. In some cases they actually increase the odds of an autocratic leaders survival in office. Citizens in autocratic countries tend to blame sender countries more than their own leadership. This perspective of foreign opression can create a rally effect around that autocratic leader.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sanction Busters are countries who interfere in the process of economic sanctions implemented by senders. For example, Iranian oil is not suppsoed to be imported by other countries in the world. However, certain countries are strategically purchasing Iranian oil to intentially ‘bust’ American sanctions there.

Targeted smart sanctions are more effective than general sanctions. These targeted sanctions can reduce negative spill-overs, these sanctions do not effect the citizens of the targeted country. Instead they target individuals in the target country.
